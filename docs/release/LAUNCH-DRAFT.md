---

Lean Model Lab makes CPU LLM efficiency experiments inspectable and reproducible: freeze a proposal, model, workload and quality rule; execute the real comparison; inspect every attempt; recover supported failures; and export the evidence.

The v1 research package brings together a quality-constrained cache benchmark, a bounded context-selection and learned-routing investigation, and a challenge to compression when the baseline already reuses context. The held-out cache benchmark records 127/128 exact answers in each arm and a 4.12× median service-throughput ratio across two balanced pairs on one shared CPU host. No response meets the frozen joint interactive-latency SLO. The other studies retain harmful omissions, failed quality gates and a learned controller that selects full context throughout confirmation. These synthetic tasks support bounded findings; they do not establish general language competence or a universal speedup.

A separate agent repeated one complete cell from each investigation, retaining 1,536 new native observations and substantial timing variation. The review reports exact agreement or discrepancies without turning a repeated cell into fresh confirmation. These checks were performed on the same shared host and do not replace external-person use or qualified human review.

The package includes source, exact experimental producers, portable workbenches, raw-output inventories, analysis scripts, figures and technical reports. Models and the native backend are separately obtained pinned dependencies. Original project material is AGPL-3.0-only.

Useful next contributions include an independently documented rerun, an accessibility review of the actual workbench, and a prospectively designed workload or bounded mechanism that challenges one of the reported limits. Keep unsuccessful attempts and unchanged quality rules in every submission.
